@ card Made for experimenting
One thing first: creative modes are new, and experimental. They can surprise you, in good ways and in odd ones. [pause:0.4s] So treat them as a playground: try a mode, listen, and keep what you like.

@ Creative modes live in the song templates
Creative modes live in the song templates. We take YuE2 Song, where they reach the furthest.

@ The template is the one from tutorial 1
It's the same template as in tutorial one. Three parts make the creative modes.

@ In the Song Brief: arrangement
First, in the Song Brief: arrangement, marked experimental. [pause:0.3s] Simple, the default, lets YuE2 plan everything by itself, just like before.

@ The creative modes: standard, varied
The creative modes: standard, varied, fantasy, sterile, many instruments, and dramatic. [pause:0.3s] Or a mode of your own, written as a small Markdown file.

@ We take “dramatic”
We take dramatic: one long arc, from a quiet start to a big last chorus.

@ genre closeness: how typical
Genre closeness sets how typical the song stays. A hundred is strictly by the book, seventy adds personal touches, forty is free within the genre, and zero borrows from any style.

@ Writer model: one choice for every writing step
Second, the Writer model: one choice for every writing step, the lyrics and the arrangement. Gemma 4 by default, or any local LLM on your machine.

@ In SCORE, the new block Arrange
Third, in the score group, the new block Arrange. After YuE2 has planned the score, it asks the writer for a section plan: chords, what the instrument line plays, the energy, and maybe a key lift.

@ The writer never writes notes
The writer never writes notes itself. Plenio writes every note, and checks the score. [pause:0.3s] If a plan can't be used, the score simply stays as it was.

@ Arrangement seed: another arrangement
And the arrangement seed gives you another arrangement of the same song.
