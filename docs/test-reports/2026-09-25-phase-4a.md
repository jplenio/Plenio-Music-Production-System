## E1 - SheetSage2 transcription and its time grid

`tools/studies/sheetsage_timeline.py` runs the native encoder along the native node's path and keeps the decoded events.

| | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | M1 | M2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ABC identical to `SheetSage2AudioToABC(full)` | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| transcription time | 58.0 s (incl. warm-up) | 39.3 s | 41.6 s | 19.5 s | 68.9 s | 46.3 s |
| bars plan → transcription | 46 → 46 | 55 → 55 | 68 → 68 | 27 → 27 | 123 | 42 |
| timeline measures = ABC bars | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| key plan → transcription | B♭ → B♭ | B♭ → B♭ | B♭ → B♭ | E♭ → E♭ | A (major; brief said A minor) | A |
| `Q:` plan → transcription | 82 → 83 | 82 → 83 | 82 → 83 | 82 → 83 | 122 (brief 126) | 121 |
| median local tempo | 82.8 | 82.5 | 82.5 | 82.8 | 121.8 | — |
| `Vocal` notes plan → transcription | 101 → 100 | 100 → 101 | 196 → 198 | 0 → 0 | 441 | 163 |
| section labels/order | = plan | = plan | = plan | = plan | intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus (×3), verse, bridge | verse, pre-chorus, chorus, outro |
| boundary differences vs plan | chorus 2/outro by 5 bars | 2 bars | 8 bars | none | post-chorus and outro not labelled | — |
| leading pickup padded to a full bar | yes (0.57 s notated as 4/4) | yes | yes | yes | yes (1 beat) | **no** (cut on a downbeat) |
| max error of a constant-tempo reading (bar starts) | 1.75 s | 1.81 s | 1.85 s | 2.13 s | 1.02 s | 0.28 s |

Findings:

1. SheetSage2 reproduces YuE2's own plans almost exactly (same bars, key, section order; ±2 vocal notes) — YuE2 renders follow the plan closely, and SheetSage2 reads them back consistently. Plan vs. take `Vocal` melody overlap (onset ±1/16, pitch class) was 0.985 for Y1 (an unrelated song in the same key: 0.13).
2. The native ABC has one `Q:` (total quarters / total seconds) but notes quantised to the real beat grid, and the first decoded beats become a padded bar. A constant-tempo reading is therefore off by up to 2.1 s — too much for bar-level lyric placement (bars are 2.9 s at 83 BPM). The decoded beat grid removes the error; it is obtainable from the same decoded events without a second transcription.
3. Section **labels and order** were right on all six files; section **boundaries** were off by 2–8 bars in three YuE2 takes (always at the last chorus/outro) — the score sheet must make fixing boundaries easy.
4. Transcription costs 20–70 s per song, more than ASR.

## E2 - ASR (faster-whisper large-v3, CTranslate2)

`tools/studies/asr_whisper.py`, GPU float16, beam 5, word timestamps, `condition_on_previous_text=False`.

| Material | Config | Language (p) | Words | Mean word p | WER | Errors (S/D/I) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 excerpt | auto | en (0.81) | 123 | 0.949 | **0.8 %** | 1/0/0 | 4.1 s |
| M1 full song | auto | en (0.90) | 336 | 0.942 | **1.2 %** | 3/0/1 | 11.4 s |
| Y1 | auto | en (0.96) | 82 | 0.897 | **8.6 %** | 6/0/1 | 4.1 s |
| Y3 | auto | en (0.91) | 155 | 0.924 | 10.8 % (**6.7 %** without the unsung stage direction) | 5/7/5 | 5.8 s |
| Y2 | auto | en (0.90) | 84 | 0.886 | 32.1 % | 23/0/3 | 3.9 s |
| Y4 instrumental | auto | **si** (0.66) | 69 / 36 (two runs) | 0.50 | — | invented text | 50–70 s |
| Y4 instrumental | en | en | 10–11 | 0.40–0.45 | — | "Thank you.", "I'm sorry.", "Thank you for watching!" | 8 s |
| Y1 | en + Silero VAD | en | **0** | — | 100 % | VAD removed all singing | 1 s |
| M2 excerpt | en, **CPU int8** | en | 123 | — | 0.8 % | 1/0/0 | 53.1 s |

Findings:

1. **VAD must be off** for singing over music.
2. On a produced pop vocal the full-mix WER is ~1 % — vocal separation brings no measurable gain on this material (D-05: separation node not built).
3. YuE2 vocals are less clear (6.7–8.6 %); the 32 % of Y2 is not an ASR error: YuE2 sang the chorus where the second verse was planned (the transcript shows the chorus three times). A **sung-lyrics check** detects this reliably.
4. Whisper hallucinates on instrumental audio (sign-off phrases, other languages) with low word probabilities; temperature fallback makes the output non-deterministic (69 vs 36 words on two runs) → Plenio uses `temperature=0` and caches results.
5. On sung takes, invented words appear **after the singing** (Y2 "Thank you." 14 s after the last lyric, Y1 "you", Y3 "you next time." with p 0.997). None of them has a SheetSage2 `Vocal` note within 6 s; the genuine last words of M1 have 16. The draft therefore leaves out short phrases outside the vocal melody (and reports them).
6. CPU int8 is a usable fallback (0.64 × real time, same WER). CTranslate2 4.8.2 on Windows needed the CUDA 12 cuBLAS from the `nvidia-cublas-cu12` wheel next to torch's cuDNN 9; without adding those folders to the DLL path it fails with "cublas64_12.dll not found".

## E2b - placing words into score sections

`tools/studies/alignment.py`: each ASR word at the bar containing its midpoint; bars → sections of the transcription; scored against the lyric sheet's sections for words that the alignment matches to the reference.

| Material | Scored words | beat grid | constant tempo | beat grid + phrase majority | **beat grid + pickup rule** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 excerpt | 122 | 95.9 % | 95.9 % | 81.2 % | **100 %** |
| M1 full song | 332 | 94.9 % | 90.7 % | 91.9 % | **99.4 %** |
| Y3 | 145 | 97.9 % | 85.5 % | 100 % | **100 %** |
| Y1 | 75 | 80.0 % | 61.3 % | 89.3 % | **89.3 %** |
| Y2 | 58 | 75.9 % | 65.5 % | 86.2 % | **86.2 %** |

- Midpoint placement fails on **pickups**: phrase-initial words sung just before a section's first downbeat ("If I'd…", "Neon…") land in the previous section.
- A phrase-majority rule fixes pickups but breaks on continuous singing (M1: a whole pre-chorus moved into the chorus).
- The **pickup rule** (a phrase that starts within one bar before the downbeat and continues into the section moves there; phrase starts = pause ≥ 0.3 s or an ASR segment start) is right in both situations.
- Remaining Y1/Y2 errors are label differences, not placement errors (words are placed where they are sung: SheetSage2's chorus/outro boundary differs from the lyric sheet; Y2 sang a chorus in the verse slot).
- The automatic draft for M2 (sections from the transcription, one line per ASR segment) matched the lyric sheet except one token join ("half -dead") — the detokenisation rule is part of the spec.

## E2c - Gemma 4 E4B as a native ASR (no extra install)

`render_matrix.py --experiment gemma_asr`: native `TrimAudioDuration` (30-s windows) → `TextGenerate` (Gemma 4 E4B fp8, greedy, audio input) with "Transcribe the lyrics that are sung in this audio clip…".

|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 excerpt | 3 | 8 s | 10.6 % | 0.8 % |
| Y1 | 5 | 8 s | 46.9 % | 8.6 % |

Gemma transcribes a clear pop vocal reasonably but YuE2 vocals poorly, and it gives no word timestamps (only 30-s windows), so it cannot place words into bars. It is **not** an ASR engine for Plenio; it remains useful as the `listen` detector (§E3).

## E4 - instrumental conditions, YuE2 text path, adapter load (AS-07)

`tools/studies/render_matrix.py --experiment instrumental`: style *"folk ballad, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, subtle piano, upright bass, brushed drums, acoustic guitar melody, intimate, sparse, 85 BPM"*; plan `full` (plan seed = take seed); if the plan had `Vocal` notes, the lead preparation (`prepare(instrumental, lead)`) was applied before rendering, as the Song path does; render with the native Plenio settings (32 steps, ceiling = score × 1.15 + 10 s). Adapter: `LoraLoader` strength_clip 1.0, strength_model 0.

| Take | Lyrics | Adapter | Plan time | Plan `Vocal` notes | Sections | Bars | Score | Rendered | Render time | Ending (tail / median loudness) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tags-s1 | `[Intro] [Verse] [Chorus] [Outro]` | off | 36 s* | **219** | 9 | 91 | 256.9 s | 254.8 s | 121 s* | 0.01 (silence) |
| tags-s2 | same | off | 21 s | **132** | 7 | 66 | 188.6 s | 184.6 s | 78 s | 0.10 |
| bare-s1 | `[instrumental]` | off | 20 s | **139** | 8 | 83 | 234.4 s | 228.4 s | 98 s | 0.00 |
| bare-s2 | same | off | 17 s | **137** | 7 | 71 | 202.9 s | 198.2 s | 83 s | 0.00 |
| tags-lora-s1 | tags | **on** | 19 s | 0 | 4 | 80 | 225.9 s | 240.0 s (+14 s) | 104 s | **1.09 (music still playing)** |
| tags-lora-s2 | tags | **on** | 45 s | 0 | 4 | 127 | 362.9 s | 427.3 s (= ceiling) | 237 s | **1.00** |
| bare-lora-s1 | bare | **on** | 29 s | 0 | 4 | 107 | 302.1 s | 357.4 s (= ceiling) | 179 s | **1.07** |
| bare-lora-s2 | bare | **on** | 87 s | — | — | — | — | not rendered | — | plan **invalid**: stopped at the planner's 8 192-token limit in the middle of group 64 (9 160 characters) |

\* includes model loading.

Findings:

1. **Adapter load (AS-07): verified.** No "lora key not loaded" line in the server log; with the same seed the plan changes completely (tags-s1 → tags-lora-s1: 219 → 0 vocal notes, 9 → 4 sections).
2. **Without the adapter the planner writes a vocal melody in 4 of 4 plans** and invents a full song form, for both tag forms. The deterministic lead preparation is therefore required, not optional (G1).
3. **With the adapter, plans have no vocal melody (3/3), but** they are long (226–363 s for four tags) or never finish (1/4 truncated at the token limit), the render runs past the plan's end (+14 s, +55 s, +64 s; two runs stopped at the render ceiling), and **all three takes end with the music at full level**. Without the adapter all renders stopped 2–6 s before the plan's end and faded to silence.
4. **Length:** with tag-only lyrics the planner ignores the intended size (four tags → 3–6 minutes). Length control for instrumental songs must be deterministic (Score Tools *fit length*, instrumental-strategy §3.2).
5. **Cost:** render time 0.42–0.55 × the rendered length; plans 17–45 s.

Vocal presence in the audio of these takes: §E3.

### E4b - timed tags (length control by conditioning)

Lyrics `[intro 0:00-0:12] [verse 0:12-0:40] [chorus 0:40-1:05] [outro 1:05-1:20]` (target 80 s), same style, otherwise as E4.

| Take | Adapter | Plan `Vocal` notes | Sections | Score | Rendered | Render time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| timed-s1 | off | 137 | intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus | 158.1 s | 156.1 s | 60 s |
| timed-s2 | off | 30 | intro, verse, outro | 62.9 s | 59.7 s | 22 s |
| timed-lora-s1 | on | 0 | intro, verse, interlude | **386.8 s** | 300.0 s (context budget reached) | 142 s |
| timed-lora-s2 | on | — | — | — | not rendered | plan **invalid**: again cut at the 8 192-token limit (group 64) |

Timed tags shorten some plans but do not control the length (63–387 s for an 80-s target). Over E4 and E4b, **2 of 6 adapter plans never finished** (token limit), the 4 valid ones had no vocal melody; **6 of 6 plans without the adapter had one** (30–219 notes). With the adapter the plan grew so long that YuE2's context budget (24 576 tokens minus prompt and ABC) ended the render at 300 s, 87 s before the plan's end — exactly the case Song Sheet · Score warns about ("the song cannot finish"). Length control stays deterministic (*fit length*).

## 8. Listening pack for the owner

All study takes are copied, numbered, to `D:\Daten2\Deepseek\ComfyUI-MiniMax\Plenio-Listening-Pack\2026-09-25-phase-4a\` (outside the repository; audio is not committed), with `README.md` listing per file the condition, what to listen for, what the detectors measured, and an empty *verdict* column. The verdicts answer what the machines cannot:

1. Do the instrumental takes contain any voice (humming, choir, spoken words, vocal chops)? → labels for the detector thresholds (Q-C5).
2. Does the adapter sound better or worse (apart from the endings)? → D-06 (AS-07 quality).
3. Lead conversion vs. accompaniment only: which is the more useful instrumental cover? → Q-C3.
4. Are the original and the new lyrics sung understandably and in the right places? → AS-18.

Reproduce: `tools/studies/listening_pack.py --evaluation <e3/evaluation.json> --takes <dev output> --source <excerpt> --out <pack>`.
