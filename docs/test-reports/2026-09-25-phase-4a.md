Findings:

1. SheetSage2 reproduces YuE2's own plans almost exactly (same bars, key, section order; ±2 vocal notes) — YuE2 renders follow the plan closely, and SheetSage2 reads them back consistently. Plan vs. take `Vocal` melody overlap (onset ±1/16, pitch class) was 0.985 for Y1 (an unrelated song in the same key: 0.13).
2. The native ABC has one `Q:` (total quarters / total seconds) but notes quantised to the real beat grid, and the first decoded beats become a padded bar. A constant-tempo reading is therefore off by up to 2.1 s — too much for bar-level lyric placement (bars are 2.9 s at 83 BPM). The decoded beat grid removes the error; it is obtainable from the same decoded events without a second transcription.
3. Section **labels and order** were right on all six files; section **boundaries** were off by 2–8 bars in three YuE2 takes (always at the last chorus/outro) — the score sheet must make fixing boundaries easy.
4. Transcription costs 20–70 s per song, more than ASR.

| M2 excerpt | en, **CPU int8** | en | 123 | — | 0.8 % | 1/0/0 | 53.1 s |

Findings:

1. **VAD must be off** for singing over music.
2. On a produced pop vocal the full-mix WER is ~1 % — vocal separation brings no measurable gain on this material (D-05: separation node not built).
3. YuE2 vocals are less clear (6.7–8.6 %); the 32 % of Y2 is not an ASR error: YuE2 sang the chorus where the second verse was planned (the transcript shows the chorus three times). A **sung-lyrics check** detects this reliably.
4. Whisper hallucinates on instrumental audio (sign-off phrases, other languages) with low word probabilities; temperature fallback makes the output non-deterministic (69 vs 36 words on two runs) → Plenio uses `temperature=0` and caches results.
5. On sung takes, invented words appear **after the singing** (Y2 "Thank you." 14 s after the last lyric, Y1 "you", Y3 "you next time." with p 0.997). None of them has a SheetSage2 `Vocal` note within 6 s; the genuine last words of M1 have 16. The draft therefore leaves out short phrases outside the vocal melody (and reports them).
6. CPU int8 is a usable fallback (0.64 × real time, same WER). CTranslate2 4.8.2 on Windows needed the CUDA 12 cuBLAS from the `nvidia-cublas-cu12` wheel next to torch's cuDNN 9; without adding those folders to the DLL path it fails with "cublas64_12.dll not found".

## E2b - placing words into score sections

`tools/studies/alignment.py`: each ASR word at the bar containing its midpoint; bars → sections of the transcription; scored against the lyric sheet's sections for words that the alignment matches to the reference.

| Material | Scored words | beat grid | constant tempo | beat grid + phrase majority | **beat grid + pickup rule** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 excerpt | 122 | 95.9 % | 95.9 % | 81.2 % | **100 %** |
| M1 full song | 332 | 94.9 % | 90.7 % | 91.9 % | **99.4 %** |
| Y3 | 145 | 97.9 % | 85.5 % | 100 % | **100 %** |
| Y1 | 75 | 80.0 % | 61.3 % | 89.3 % | **89.3 %** |
| Y2 | 58 | 75.9 % | 65.5 % | 86.2 % | **86.2 %** |

- Midpoint placement fails on **pickups**: phrase-initial words sung just before a section's first downbeat ("If I'd…", "Neon…") land in the previous section.
- A phrase-majority rule fixes pickups but breaks on continuous singing (M1: a whole pre-chorus moved into the chorus).
- The **pickup rule** (a phrase that starts within one bar before the downbeat and continues into the section moves there; phrase starts = pause ≥ 0.3 s or an ASR segment start) is right in both situations.
- Remaining Y1/Y2 errors are label differences, not placement errors (words are placed where they are sung: SheetSage2's chorus/outro boundary differs from the lyric sheet; Y2 sang a chorus in the verse slot).
- The automatic draft for M2 (sections from the transcription, one line per ASR segment) matched the lyric sheet except one token join ("half -dead") — the detokenisation rule is part of the spec.

## E2d - ASR only where the transcription has vocal notes

`asr_whisper.py --config regions --regions <e1>`: the merged intervals of SheetSage2's `Vocal` notes (gaps under 2 s merged, 0.5 s margin) are passed to faster-whisper as `clip_timestamps`; default temperature fallback with a fixed CTranslate2 seed (`regions`) and temperature 0 (`regions_t0`) gave identical results.

| Material | WER full audio (E2) | WER vocal regions | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 | 8.6 % | 8.6 % | trailing "you" gone |
| Y2 | 32.1 % | 29.6 % | trailing "Thank you." gone (the rest is the swapped section) |
| Y3 | 10.8 % | **7.6 %** | trailing "you next time." gone |
| Y4 (instrumental) | 10–69 invented words | **not transcribed** (no vocal notes) | — |
| M1 | 1.2 % | 1.5 % | +1 word at a region edge |
| M2 | 0.8 % | 1.6 % | +1 word at a region edge |

Also measured (E3 data): with `temperature=0` on **full** instrumental audio Whisper detected Sinhala/Shona and looped (e.g. 304 × "අපි"); with explicit English it produced sign-off phrases ("Thank you for watching!", "I'll see you next time."). Decoding with temperature 0 alone is therefore no safeguard; restricting ASR to the vocal regions is. The margin (0.5 s here) is tuned in 4B against the edge errors.

1. **`score` and `listen` agree:** every sung file is flagged by both (≥ 100 notes; 3/3 windows). Of the 24 intended-instrumental takes, `score` flags 4 (22–173 notes) and `listen` 3 of the same 4; the other 20 have 0–2 notes and 0/3 windows. The one disagreement (e4-tags-s2: 65 notes, 23 s of them inside Gemma's windows, but 0/3) is either a vocal-like guitar lead read as `Vocal` or a voice Gemma missed — the owner's verdict decides.
2. **`words` is unusable as a vocal detector:** Whisper invents 100–480 "confident" words on instrumental takes (loops in Sinhala with automatic language, sign-off phrases in English). It stays an ASR, not a detector.
3. **Every flagged take was rendered without the adapter** (4 of 13 non-adapter takes vs. 0 of 10 adapter takes). Two of them are text-path takes whose (lead-prepared) score had no sounding `Vocal` note — the deterministic preparation (G1) does not prevent YuE2 from singing the lead line (G4); one is *accompaniment only + new harmony*, where the score is almost empty and YuE2 filled it with a vocal-like melody.
4. It runs only because Windows lets CUDA spill into shared system memory; a 357-s take (two native windows) failed with *CUDA out of memory* after 29 GiB. Whether a 16 GB Linux card (no spill) transcribes at all is unverified (AS-20).

Thresholds proposed for *Check Vocals* (instrumental-strategy §6.2): `score` flags at ≥ 10 `Vocal` notes or ≥ 5 s of them per take; `listen` flags at any *yes* window, with windows tiling the whole take. Both stay marked *uncalibrated* until the owner's verdicts confirm them.

1. **Adapter load (AS-07): verified.** No "lora key not loaded" line in the server log; with the same seed the plan changes completely (tags-s1 → tags-lora-s1: 219 → 0 vocal notes, 9 → 4 sections).

1. **Covers keep the source.** Sung and lead covers keep the melody (overlap 0.75–0.93 vs. 0.13 baseline), all takes keep the key, and — except three non-adapter outliers — bar count and tempo. *Harmony keep* keeps the chords (0.81–0.95); *harmony new* changes them (0.24–0.57), as intended.
2. **Original lyrics work end-to-end without manual help:** the automatic ASR draft (E2b), unchanged, was sung back with 1–3 % WER. New lyrics with the same line structure: 20 % WER — sung, with some words changed or dropped (listening: owner).
3. **Adapter in covers: no downside measured, several upsides.** With the adapter all six instrumental covers stayed at the score length (82.8–84.9 s), none was flagged by either vocal detector, and melody overlap was equal or higher. Without it, 3 of 7 instrumental covers left the score (+16 s, +22.5 s to the ceiling, −16 s) and 2 of 7 were flagged as vocal (§E3). The text-path problems of the adapter (E4) do not occur here because covers do not plan: the score is given.
4. **Accompaniment only + new harmony** leaves an almost empty score; YuE2 then improvised freely (0.05 overlap), ran to the render ceiling and was flagged as vocal by both detectors — the Cover Brief warning is justified.
5. Render time 23–46 s for 83 s of audio (0.3–0.55 × real time); covers need no planning step.
6. Lead conversion policy *replace* dropped no note on this source (the source's `Ins` line rarely overlaps the vocal); a source with a busy instrumental melody is needed to compare *replace* and *keep Ins* (Q-C3, 4B).
